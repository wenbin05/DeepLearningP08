\section{Abstract}
We investigate whether LoRA placement in BART-base's encoder self-attention,
decoder self-attention, or encoder--decoder cross-attention affects summarization
quality under exactly matched trainable-parameter budgets. With rank $r=8$ and
query/value projections, each isolated region introduces 147,456 trainable
parameters. The implementation now supports controlled CNN/DailyMail training,
validation, and held-out CNN/DailyMail and XSum evaluation, with saved configurations
and official ROUGE metrics. No genuine training logs, checkpoints, or per-seed
results were present in the audited repository. Consequently, the earlier draft's
scores and qualitative examples are unverified and are not presented as findings.
The main outstanding work is to execute the baseline and placement matrix with
three independent seeds, evaluate transfer without further tuning, and conduct
the human factual-consistency study.

\section{Introduction and Problem Statement}
Abstractive summarization compresses lengthy source documents into fluent, salient, and factually accurate summaries. Pre-trained sequence-to-sequence Transformers such as BART~\cite{lewis2020bart} achieve high ROUGE scores by leveraging bidirectional encoder representations coupled with autoregressive decoder generation. However, conventional adaptation requires updating all 139.4 million parameters in BART-base, incurring steep computational overhead and high storage requirements when maintaining separate checkpoints for multiple domains or tasks. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, particularly Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)~\cite{hu2022lora}, circumvent this cost by freezing the pre-trained weights $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ and injecting trainable rank-decomposition matrices $\Delta W = B A$.

\textbf{Scope and Rubric Alignment.} The primary extension is the matched-budget
placement experiment. The supplied P08 guide also requests full fine-tuning,
Prefix Tuning, rank and projection ablations, cross-domain evaluation, and a

\textbf{Research Question.} Under strictly matched trainable parameter budgets and identical training conditions, how does the architectural placement of LoRA adapters (encoder self-attention vs. decoder self-attention vs. encoder-decoder cross-attention) govern summarization performance, factual consistency, and zero-shot cross-domain generalization?

\section{Related Work (Updated)}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning for conditional text generation has evolved rapidly, transitioning from uniform parameter perturbation to structural and capacity-aware allocation.

\textbf{Parameter-Efficient Sequence-to-Sequence Modeling.} Large-scale pre-training of sequence-to-sequence architectures, exemplified by BART~\cite{lewis2020bart}, established denoising objectives as the foundation for modern summarization. While full fine-tuning updates all parameters, Prefix-Tuning~\cite{li2021prefix} introduced continuous virtual prefix vectors prepended to attention keys and values, preserving frozen model weights while achieving competitive generation on XSum with a small task-specific parameter budget. Concurrently, LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} demonstrated that task-specific parameter updates exhibit low intrinsic dimensionality, parametrizing weight updates via low-rank matrices $\Delta W = BA$. In their foundational work, Hu et al. observed that adapting attention query and value projections suffices for autoregressive language models. Its benchmarks include both language understanding and generation; our experiment specifically tests architectural regions within BART.

\textbf{Non-Uniform Capacity Allocation and Layer Sensitivity.} Recognizing that uniform low-rank parameterization across all layers may be sub-optimal, recent research has explored dynamic allocation. AdaLoRA~\cite{zhang2023adalora} parameterizes weight matrices using singular value decomposition and iteratively prunes singular values based on importance scores, allocating higher rank to influential matrices. Similarly, WeightLoRA~\cite{veprikov2026weightlora} evaluates adapter significance during fine-tuning, demonstrating that adapters are not uniformly useful throughout BART. In a broader theoretical analysis, He et al.~\cite{he2022unified} established a unified framework connecting adapters, prefix tuning, and LoRA, comparing the design choices shared by several PEFT methods. Furthermore, Valipour et al.~\cite{valipour2023dylora} introduced DyLoRA to train models across a spectrum of ranks dynamically, supporting selection among multiple ranks without separately training every rank. While these methods demonstrate parameter non-uniformity, they optimize allocation dynamically via complex heuristics, leaving open the empirical question of how distinct, interpretable architectural blocks perform under an identical, static budget.

\textbf{Factual Consistency in Abstractive Summarization.} A known limitation of n-gram overlap metrics such as ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L is their insensitivity to factual hallucinations. Kryscinski et al.~\cite{kryscinski2020evaluating} demonstrated that high-ROUGE summaries frequently introduce factual inconsistencies unsupported by the source text, proposing FactCC as a weakly supervised evaluation metric. Automatic factuality assessment also has domain limitations. We plan to complement ROUGE with a human study; neither a FactCC experiment nor human judgments are available in this repository.

\section{Methodology}
Our methodology enforces strict experimental consistency to reduce confounding when assessing whether performance differences originate from adapter placement rather than training variations or capacity disparities.

\subsection{Model Architecture and Placement Isolation}
We use pre-trained \texttt{facebook/bart-base} as our backbone, which comprises an encoder with 6 Transformer layers and a decoder with 6 Transformer layers ($d_{\text{model}} = 768$, 12 attention heads, feed-forward dimension $d_{\text{ff}} = 3072$, totaling 139,420,416 parameters). For any weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$, LoRA parameterizes the forward pass as:
\begin{equation}
h = W_0 x + \Delta W x = W_0 x + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A x
\end{equation}
where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$ uses PEFT's default Kaiming-uniform initialization, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ is initialized to zero, $r$ is the adaptation rank, and $\alpha$ is a scaling hyperparameter. We set $r = 8$ and $\alpha = 16$, targeting the query ($W_q$) and value ($W_v$) projections ($k = d = 768$). Each adapted projection introduces $2 \times 768 \times 8 = 12,288$ parameters.

We construct four distinct LoRA configurations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Standard LoRA (All Attention):} Injects adapters into $W_q$ and $W_v$ across all 6 encoder self-attention, 6 decoder self-attention, and 6 decoder cross-attention blocks (18 attention layers total $\times 24,576 = 442,368$ trainable parameters, $0.32\%$ of the base model).
  \item \textbf{Encoder Self-Attention LoRA:} Adapts only \texttt{model.encoder.layers.*.self\_attn} ($6 \times 24,576 = 147,456$ trainable parameters, $0.11\%$).
  \item \textbf{Decoder Self-Attention LoRA:} Adapts only \texttt{model.decoder.layers.*.self\_attn} ($6 \times 24,576 = 147,456$ trainable parameters, $0.11\%$).
  \item \textbf{Cross-Attention LoRA:} Adapts only \texttt{model.decoder.layers.*.encoder\_attn} ($6 \times 24,576 = 147,456$ trainable parameters, $0.11\%$).
\end{enumerate}
Crucially, configurations (2), (3), and (4) share an identical parameter budget of exactly 147,456 parameters, controlling adapter capacity in the architectural comparison.

\subsection{Dataset and Preprocessing}
We prepare in-domain summarization on the CNN/DailyMail dataset (Version 3.0.0), comprising 287,113 training, 13,368 validation, and 11,490 test document-summary pairs. Source news stories are truncated to a maximum of 1,024 BPE tokens, and target multi-sentence summaries are capped at 128 tokens. Special tokens (\texttt{<s>}, \texttt{</s>}) are prepended and appended, with target padding tokens replaced by $-100$ to exclude them from cross-entropy loss computation. For zero-shot cross-domain evaluation, we use the test split of XSum (226,711 total samples: 204,045 train, 11,332 validation, 11,334 test), which features highly abstractive, single-sentence summaries of BBC news articles.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
The default pilot uses a fixed, shuffled 10,000-example CNN/DailyMail training
subset (data seed 42) and the full official validation split. The data seed stays
fixed while training seeds vary over 42, 123, and 999. The final checkpoint is
selected by epoch/update count, not by test performance. Test runs load that
checkpoint and evaluate the full official CNN/DailyMail and XSum test splits
without training. Hub revision SHAs, split sizes, selected-example hashes,
generation settings, package versions, and model configuration are saved.

ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L use Hugging Face Evaluate's official ROUGE
implementation with stemming and arithmetic mean per-example F1, multiplied by
100. ROUGE-L is distinct from ROUGE-Lsum. References for ROUGE retain their full
original text; training and loss labels are truncated to 128 tokens. Dynamic label
padding uses $-100$. The generation settings in Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams} apply
to both test domains. Keeping the CNN/DailyMail minimum generation length of 56
on single-sentence XSum is a protocol choice that may disadvantage transfer;
any alternative should be declared as a separate evaluation ablation.

The aggregation script requires one completed run for each expected seed and
rejects duplicates, missing seeds, or incompatible within-configuration protocols.
It computes mean and sample standard deviation ($n-1$ denominator) from saved
results. Full fine-tuning, Prefix Tuning, and all-attention LoRA are comparison
baselines, not matched-parameter placement variants. Equal examples and updates
do not imply equal FLOPs or wall-clock compute; timing and update counts must
be considered when assessing the guide's matched-compute objective.

\section{Challenges and Adaptations}
The audit found that training did not connect ROUGE to the Trainer, an evaluation
fallback invented an approximation for ROUGE-L, and the plotting script contained
hard-coded scores. Generation settings were only partially applied, metrics and
final models were not explicitly saved for downstream evaluation, and test-set
and XSum paths were absent. These implementation defects are addressed by shared
model construction, official metrics with failures propagated, explicit generation
configuration, persistent run manifests, and evaluation-only checkpoint loading.

The previous draft's claims of observed CUDA out-of-memory failures, successful
GPU tuning, and earlier unit-test verification were not accompanied by logs or
tests. They are not retained as observations. Actual checks and their outcomes
are recorded in the repository's audit documentation. Full training requires
separate execution; smoke-test outputs do not constitute research results.

Baseline reproduction and preliminary ablations are necessary for the midterm
rubric's quantitative-results criteria. A functional implementation alone does
not satisfy those criteria. The exact faculty-approved scope, any external logs,
